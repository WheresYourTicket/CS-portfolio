CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250 at Southern New Hampshire University. Throughout this course, I explored the Agile development process and the responsibilities of different Scrum Team members, including the Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developer, and Tester. The final project reflects what I learned about teamwork, communication, and adapting to changes during software development.

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

Understanding what the user actually needs is one of the most important parts of software development. Before starting development, I think it's important to communicate with stakeholders and understand what problem they're trying to solve. User stories help break those needs into smaller, manageable tasks that explain what the user wants and why it's important. They also help developers and testers understand what needs to be accomplished. Throughout this course, I found the Product Owner role particularly interesting because it focuses on understanding customer needs and making sure the development team is working on the right priorities.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I prefer approaching development by breaking larger projects into smaller tasks that can be completed and tested along the way. One thing I appreciate about Agile is that it allows teams to make adjustments without having to restart the entire project. In my experience working in IT, requirements and priorities can change as new problems come up, so having that flexibility is important. Going forward, I would like to incorporate sprint planning, backlog management, and regular retrospectives into my work. I also think using tools like Jira to track progress and organize tasks can help teams stay focused and accountable.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means more than just completing your assigned work. It also means communicating with others, being willing to help when problems come up, and taking responsibility for your contributions. Throughout this course, I learned how important communication is between the Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers, and Testers. Even when everyone understands their responsibilities, a project can still fall behind if the team isn't working together. I think a good team member should be dependable, open to feedback, and willing to adapt when the team needs to change direction.
